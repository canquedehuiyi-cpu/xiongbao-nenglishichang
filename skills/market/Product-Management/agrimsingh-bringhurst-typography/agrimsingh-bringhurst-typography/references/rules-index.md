## Chapter 1: Grand Design
- `1.1.1` Honor content.
- `1.1.2` Honor the life and dignity of letters.
- `1.1.3` Work toward style beyond surface style.
- `1.2.1` Read text before designing.
- `1.2.2` Derive outer form from inner textual logic.
- `1.2.3` Reflect real relationships among text, images, notes, and tables.
- `1.2.4` Choose faces that clarify character and voice.
- `1.2.5` Shape page and text block to reveal logic and nuance.
- `1.2.6` Give incidental details full typographic attention.

## Chapter 2: Rhythm and Proportion
- `2.1.1` Define word space for size and natural fit.
- `2.1.2` Choose comfortable measure.
- `2.1.3` Set ragged when text and page benefit.
- `2.1.4` Use single word spaces between sentences.
- `2.1.5` Keep minimal spacing in strings of initials.
- `2.1.6` Letterspace capitals, small caps, and long digit strings.
- `2.1.7` Do not letterspace lowercase without reason.
- `2.1.8` Kern consistently and modestly.
- `2.1.9` Do not distort letter widths or shapes casually.
- `2.1.10` Do not over-stretch spacing.
- `2.2.1` Choose leading to suit face, text, and measure.
- `2.2.2` Change vertical spacing in measured intervals.
- `2.2.3` Avoid suffocating the page.
- `2.3.1` Set opening paragraphs flush left.
- `2.3.2` Indent subsequent paragraphs in continuous text.
- `2.3.3` Add lead around block quotations.
- `2.3.4` Indent or center verse quotations.
- `2.4.1` Hyphenate with balanced split lengths.
- `2.4.2` Avoid weak stub endings at paragraph tails.
- `2.4.3` Avoid long runs of consecutive hyphenated lines.
- `2.4.4` Hyphenate proper names only when necessary.
- `2.4.5` Follow language-specific hyphenation conventions.
- `2.4.6` Use hard spaces for short numeric/math expressions.
- `2.4.7` Avoid repeated line starts with same word.
- `2.4.8` Avoid page-start widows from multi-line paragraphs.
- `2.4.9` Balance facing pages carefully.
- `2.4.10` Avoid awkward hyphen breaks at interruptions.
- `2.4.11` Break rules when text needs demand it.

## Chapter 3: Harmony and Counterpoint
- `3.1.1` Compose with a scale, not arbitrary sizes.
- `3.2.1` Use titling figures with full caps; text figures otherwise.
- `3.2.2` Use spaced small caps for acronyms in running text.
- `3.2.3` Resolve typographic disputes by language and thought.
- `3.3.1` Use ligatures and language-required characters properly.
- `3.3.2` If avoiding ligatures, choose faces that support that choice.
- `3.4.1` Accept cultural assumptions carried by text and type.
- `3.4.2` Do not use extra fonts without purpose.
- `3.4.3` Use sloped/artificial sloped romans sparingly.
- `3.5.1` Change one parameter at a time.
- `3.5.2` Keep foreground clear of clutter.

## Chapter 4: Structural Forms and Devices
- `4.1.1` Make title page embody text presence and dignity.
- `4.1.2` Do not let titles oppress text.
- `4.1.3` Set openings that reinforce overall design.
- `4.1.4` Mark each beginning and resumption of text.
- `4.1.5` Preserve opening quotation marks when needed.
- `4.2.1` Make heading forms support whole-system style.
- `4.2.2` Use only necessary heading levels.
- `4.3.1` Choose optimal note form (foot, end, side) for use case.
- `4.3.2` Check superscript weight and spacing.
- `4.3.3` Use superscripts in text, full-size numbers in notes.
- `4.3.4` Prevent note numbering and placement ambiguity.
- `4.4.1` Edit tables as carefully as prose; set for reading.
- `4.4.2` Avoid over-punctuating lists.
- `4.4.3` Align figures flush right or on decimal.
- `4.4.4` Choose legible, harmonious tabular alignments.
- `4.5.1` Leave adequate opening and closing space.
- `4.5.2` Give prelim sections adequate room.
- `4.5.3` Balance front and back matter.

## Chapter 5: Analphabetic Symbols
- `5.1.1` Use midpoint conventions appropriately.
- `5.1.2` Keep symbols/diacritics in tune with chosen face.
- `5.1.3` Prefer high-quality ampersands in heads/titles.
- `5.1.4` Treat hyphen design and usage carefully.
- `5.2.1` Prefer spaced en dashes for parenthetic breaks.
- `5.2.2` Use close en dashes or 3-to-em dashes for ranges.
- `5.2.3` Use em dash conventions for dialogue where appropriate.
- `5.2.4` Use 3-em repetition rule in lists/bibliographies when needed.
- `5.2.5` Distinguish virgule and solidus use.
- `5.2.6` Use dimension sign for dimensions.
- `5.2.7` Use ellipses that fit the font.
- `5.2.8` Treat punctuation as notation, not emotional decoration.
- `5.3.1` Use strong bracket/parenthesis forms with proper spacing.
- `5.3.2` Keep brackets/parentheses upright even in italic context.
- `5.4.1` Minimize unnecessary quotation marks.
- `5.4.2` Position quotes consistently against punctuation.
- `5.4.3` Omit apostrophes in numeric plurals.
- `5.4.4` Remove unnecessary punctuation.
- `5.4.5` Preserve/add punctuation when clarity requires.
- `5.5.1` Apply required accents and alternate sorts.
- `5.5.2` Remap tooling/input methods to support needed glyphs.

## Chapter 6: Choosing and Combining Type
- `6.1.1` Respect the medium a face was designed for.
- `6.1.2` Use faithful digital revivals of letterpress faces.
- `6.1.3` Choose faces robust under final production conditions.
- `6.1.4` Match faces and paper/stock characteristics.
- `6.2.1` Match face to task as well as subject.
- `6.2.2` Ensure face supports required special effects/features.
- `6.2.3` Use available resources to best advantage.
- `6.3.1` Match historical associations to text context.
- `6.3.2` Let the face speak in its natural idiom.
- `6.4.1` Match face spirit and character to content.
- `6.5.1` Start with a single family.
- `6.5.2` Respect roman/italic/small-cap integrity.
- `6.5.3` Judge bold on its own merits.
- `6.5.4` Choose titling/display faces that reinforce structure.
- `6.5.5` Pair serif and sans by inner structure, not trend.
- `6.6.1` Choose non-Latin faces with equal care.
- `6.6.2` Match typographic continuity to thought continuity.
- `6.6.3` Balance scripts optically more than mathematically.
- `6.7.1` Add no unnecessary characters.
- `6.7.2` Add only visually distinct needed characters.
- `6.7.3` Avoid arbitrary redefinition of familiar characters.
- `6.7.4` Avoid haphazard face mixing when specialized sorts needed.
- `6.8.1` Build face library slowly and deliberately.

## Chapter 8: Shaping the Page
- `8.3.1` Prefer inherently satisfying page proportions.
- `8.3.2` Match proportions to content, size, ambition.
- `8.3.3` Consider historical associations of proportions.
- `8.4.1` Use columns taller than wide for sustained reading.
- `8.4.2` Balance text block shape against page shape.
- `8.5.1` Bring margins into the design system.
- `8.5.2` Bring design logic into margins.
- `8.5.3` Mark reader wayfinding clearly.
- `8.5.4` Do not restate obvious navigation info.
- `8.6.1` Use modular scale when subdivision is needed.
- `8.8.1` Iterate between improvisation and calculation.
- `8.8.2` Use typographic increments for text; free proportion for open space.
- `8.8.3` Keep page design supple for real text behavior.

## Chapter 9: State of the Art
- `9.2.1` Distinguish character sets from glyph sets.
- `9.2.2` Use manual/random/programmed glyph variation intentionally.
- `9.2.3` Prefer contextual intelligence in type behavior when available.
- `9.5.1` Design screen text for screen constraints.
- `9.5.2` Verify type at each production stage.
- `9.5.3` Follow work through final print/output.
- `9.6.1` Consult historical exemplars.
- `9.6.2` Maintain low-tech and high-tech craft discipline.

## Chapter 10: Specimen Books
- `10.1.1` Name type accurately whenever possible.
